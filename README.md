# IP-44 Beyond abiotic drivers: how benthic vegetation and seasonality shape coastal fish larvae communities 

Coastal fish are socio-economically and culturally important, whilst playing a significant role in the food web. Environmental changes can affect their larval productivity and community composition either directly or by impacting benthic habitats, consequently affecting fish stock sustainability and predators dependent on them. However, drivers of juvenile fish communities and their ecosystem functions are rarely explored in relation to abiotic factors, bottom habitat diversity, and seasonality. To do so, we evaluated the relative importance of the soft and hard substrate vegetation, abiotic factors, and seasonality on the occurrence and abundance of the Baltic herring, pikeperch, European perch, European smelt, and gobies larvae, as well as the resulting total fish larvae availability provided by them in the northern Baltic Sea, from spring to early summer. Our Bayesian species distribution models showed that while abiotic factors were the most important drivers of larval productivity, bottom vegetation also had a significant role in shaping them. The functional diversity of aquatic vegetation had a stronger effect on larval abundance than occurrence, likely by supporting broader dietary niches and higher quality habitat. Importantly, species specific larval abundances expressed significant asynchronous within-year variations resulting in rather stable total fish larvae availability. The relative importance of the drivers of larval availability varied between the five fish species, reflecting the species-specific phenological timings and habitat preferences. Thus, our results highlight the importance of incorporating functional diversity into habitat quality assessments and encourage the addition of benthic habitat into coastal species distribution models.
